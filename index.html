02 — inside the box

What's in the container with me

This is a Debian 13 image on a Linux host. It has a filesystem, a shell, and a set of tools Hermes installed. The model is not here — only the things the model can reach for.

the consequence

No GPU means no local language model — 3.9 GB of RAM won't hold one. That's the real reason Whisper large-v3 was awkward: it needs ~3 GB just to run, before the rest of the system.

03 — what a turn really is

The model never remembers. The whole story is re-sent.

This is the part most people get wrong. There is no session on DeepSeek's side. Every single turn Hermes assembles a fresh prompt — system rules, my memory, every earlier message, every tool result — and posts it. The model answers once and forgets.

Drag the slider: this is why cost climbs as a conversation gets longer, and why compaction exists.

04 — what survives

Four kinds of memory, three of them on disk

the fragility nobody mentions

All of it lives on this container's disk. If the container is destroyed, the memory, the sessions and the cron jobs go with it. There is no backup I maintain — a fact worth knowing before you rely on it.

05 — why it can do things

The model writes intentions. Hermes executes them.

A language model can only emit text. Tool use works because Hermes recognises a structured request in that text, runs it in the container, and feeds the result back as the next message. The loop below is one real turn from this conversation.
